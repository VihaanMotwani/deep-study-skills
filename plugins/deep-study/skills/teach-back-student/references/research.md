# Research basis for Teach-Back Student

Use this reference to explain or audit the pedagogy. Do not recite studies during ordinary role-play.

## Learning by teaching

Kobayashi’s meta-analysis found positive effects from preparing to teach and larger effects from teaching after preparation. Interactive teaching showed larger effects than noninteractive teaching.

- Kobayashi, K. (2019). “Learning by preparing-to-teach and teaching: A meta-analysis.”
- https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jpr.12221

Product implication: the AI should interact, ask questions, and attempt to use what was taught. A silent audience misses part of the mechanism.

## Reflective knowledge-building

Roscoe and Chi distinguish productive tutoring behaviors from simple knowledge-telling. Explanations and questions can prompt tutors to monitor comprehension, integrate knowledge, and elaborate their mental models.

- Roscoe, R. D., & Chi, M. T. H. (2007). “Understanding Tutor Learning: Knowledge-Building and Knowledge-Telling in Peer Tutors’ Explanations and Questions.”
- https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ782047

Product implication: ask questions about relationships and implications that require the learner to build knowledge, not merely repeat definitions.

## Teachable agents

Betty’s Brain has learners teach a simulated student by constructing a causal map. Betty reasons from the learner-built model and attempts quizzes, while a separate mentor supplies evaluation. Learners inspect the result, revise the model, and try again.

- Biswas et al., overview and classroom discussion of Betty’s Brain.
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6473007/

Product implication: keep the student believable by separating its provisional understanding from transparent accuracy feedback.

SimStudent research likewise indicates that teachable agents benefit from adaptive and metacognitive scaffolding.

- https://www.simstudent.org/publications/aied2018
- https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1054420

Product implication: an AI merely acting confused is insufficient. The interaction needs bounded questions, application attempts, feedback, and repair.

## Chunk and check

Clinical teach-back is not identical to learning by teaching: the practitioner asks a patient to explain instructions in their own words. Its “chunk and check” practice nevertheless illustrates why consequential misunderstanding is often repaired after a manageable segment rather than postponed until the very end.

- Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, “Use the Teach-Back Method.”
- https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html

Product implication: use natural accuracy checkpoints. Do not interrupt every sentence or allow a foundational error to structure the entire remaining session.

## Evidence boundary

The exact conversational persona and checkpoint wording are product-design inferences, not directly validated protocols. Evaluate whether the student feels genuinely curious, whether questions make the learner elaborate, and whether misconceptions are repaired without collapsing into conventional tutoring.
