# Research basis for Guided Tutor

Use this reference to explain or audit the pedagogical choices. Do not cite it mechanically during ordinary tutoring.

## Worked examples, self-explanation, and fading

Atkinson, Renkl, and Merrill studied the transition from worked examples to independent problem solving. Across two experiments, combining self-explanation prompts with faded worked-out steps improved near and far transfer without additional time on task.

- Atkinson, R. K., Renkl, A., & Merrill, M. M. (2003). “Transitioning from Studying Examples to Solving Problems: Effects of Self-Explanation Prompts and Fading Worked-Out Steps.”
- https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ678596

Product implication: model a representative solution, ask the learner to explain important steps, and then remove completed steps until the learner solves independently.

## Cognitive engagement

The ICAP framework distinguishes passive, active, constructive, and interactive engagement. Generating explanations, predictions, and inferences is more cognitively constructive than only receiving information.

- Chi, M. T. H., & Wylie, R. (2014). “The ICAP Framework: Linking Cognitive Engagement to Active Learning Outcomes.”
- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823

Product implication: explanations should regularly lead to learner generation or dialogue, but activity should serve understanding rather than become constant quizzing.

## Retrieval practice

Roediger and Karpicke found that retrieving studied prose improved delayed retention relative to repeated study. Repeated study could look better on an immediate test, while retrieval produced stronger retention after longer delays.

- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). “Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention.”
- https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x

Product implication: end cycles with reconstruction from memory, not another summary shown to the learner.

## Expertise reversal and adaptivity

Instructional support that helps novices can become redundant for advanced learners. A recent meta-analysis found that low-prior-knowledge learners benefited more from high assistance, whereas high-prior-knowledge learners benefited more from lower assistance.

- Wiedbusch et al. (2025). “A cornerstone of adaptivity – A meta-analysis of the expertise reversal effect.”
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000660

Product implication: diagnose through a small amount of learner performance, then fade or restore support responsively. Do not force every learner through the same sequence.

## Evidence boundary

No single study validates the complete conversational routine in this skill. The routine is an evidence-informed synthesis of worked examples, self-explanation, fading, retrieval, and adaptive support. Treat learner response and domain constraints as part of the instructional decision.
