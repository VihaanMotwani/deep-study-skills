# Research basis for Study Companion

Use this reference to explain or audit the checkpoint design. Do not recite research during an ordinary study session.

## Retrieval before review

Roediger and Karpicke compared repeated study with repeated retrieval of educationally relevant prose. Restudy could produce better performance after a few minutes, while retrieval produced stronger retention after two days and one week.

- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). “Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention.”
- https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x

A meta-analysis of practice testing found that practice tests generally improve learning relative to non-testing conditions, while outcomes depend on design choices such as feedback and test format.

- Adesope, O. O., Trevisan, D. A., & Sundararajan, N. (2017). “Rethinking the Use of Tests: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Testing.”
- https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306

Product implication: ask the learner to reconstruct before showing a summary. Treat the checkpoint as learning practice, not merely assessment.

## Constructive engagement

The ICAP framework predicts deeper learning when learners generate new inferences or interact around ideas rather than only receive information.

- Chi, M. T. H., & Wylie, R. (2014). “The ICAP Framework: Linking Cognitive Engagement to Active Learning Outcomes.”
- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823

Product implication: follow recall with explanation, application, comparison, or prediction. Do not reduce the checkpoint to recognition questions.

## Feedback timing

Retrieval is most useful when errors can later be corrected. In this skill, the learner attempts recall first, then compares against the source and repairs the model. This sequencing protects the generation benefit without leaving misconceptions unaddressed.

## Evidence boundary

The research supports retrieval practice and constructive engagement. It does not establish that every learner needs a fixed-duration Pomodoro, automatic interruption, or a particular conversational persona. Waiting for the learner to return is a product choice that keeps the initial skill portable and avoids unsupported scheduling claims.
