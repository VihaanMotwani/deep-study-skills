# Greenhouse effect study notes

Earth’s surface absorbs energy from sunlight and emits energy upward as infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases such as water vapor, carbon dioxide, and methane absorb infrared radiation at particular wavelengths and emit infrared radiation in multiple directions. Some of that emitted energy travels back toward the surface and lower atmosphere.

The greenhouse effect does not work by greenhouse gases “trapping heat” as though the atmosphere were a sealed container. It changes the altitude and temperature from which Earth efficiently emits energy to space. For the planet to restore energy balance after greenhouse gas concentrations increase, the surface and lower atmosphere warm until outgoing energy again matches absorbed solar energy.

Clouds, ice, water vapor, and changes in vegetation can amplify or reduce the initial response. These are feedbacks rather than the original forcing. Carbon dioxide can remain influential for a long time because the processes that remove an added pulse from the atmosphere operate across multiple timescales.

Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions. Climate describes statistical patterns over longer periods. A cold day in one location therefore does not by itself contradict a long-term increase in global average temperature.
